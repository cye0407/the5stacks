## Voice Definitions

### Substack — "The one who did the work"

You're not lecturing. You're sharing what you found after a decade inside the problem. The tone is: "I built this, it didn't work, here's why, here's what actually works." Longer pieces. Original thinking. The place where your best ideas live in full form.

Rules:
- Every piece should make someone think "I never looked at it that way"
- No jargon unless you immediately explain it
- Write like a smart friend who runs a business, not a sustainability consultant
- Link to the book and baseline tool naturally, not as a hard sell
- Occasionally cross-promote a catyeldi.com essay when it connects
- End every piece with a question or a provocation, not a CTA

Audience: people who already sense something is broken about sustainability but haven't found anyone who articulates why. They subscribe because you think clearly, not because you sell something.

### LinkedIn — "Your operations therapist"

Never start with "I." Start with "you" or start with a situation they recognize. Every post validates their experience first, then reframes it. The 5 Stacks shows up as the answer to their problem, not your product.

Rules:
- First line must hook. LinkedIn truncates after ~2 lines. If the first line doesn't make them click "see more," the post is dead.
- No "I'm excited to announce" energy. Ever.
- Speak to SME owner-operators, operations leads, supply chain people
- The pain points: wasted consultant money, reports nobody reads, sustainability that doesn't survive budget reviews, questionnaires they can't answer, certifications that changed nothing
- CTA is always soft: "the book is free at the5stacks.com" or "→ the5stacks.com" at the end. Not "BUY MY THING"
- Engage with comments. This is where relationships start.

Audience: burned-out SME owners, skeptical operations leads, sustainability practitioners who know the current model is broken. Also: potential workshop clients, consulting partners, collaborators.

### Bluesky — "The contrarian with receipts"

Short. Sharp. Quotable. This is where you say the thing everyone is thinking but nobody is posting. Mix of:
- Found quotes about sustainability failure, corporate greenwashing, broken systems
- Your own one-liners extracted from the book or your thinking
- Pop culture references that connect to sustainability/business themes
- Hot takes on sustainability news
- Occasional links to Substack pieces

Rules:
- Under 300 characters is ideal. Bluesky rewards brevity.
- No hashtags (Bluesky culture doesn't use them heavily)
- Be funny when you can. Be angry when you should.
- Quote-tweet (repost with comment) sustainability news with your take
- Don't sell. Build a persona that people follow because you're interesting.
- The bio links to the5stacks.com. That's enough.

Audience: sustainability-adjacent people, business skeptics, anyone who follows sharp commentary on broken systems. This grows slowly but compounds.

### Threads — "Bluesky's louder cousin"

Cross-post everything from Bluesky. Same content, different audience. Threads' advantage: Meta's algorithm actively pushes content to non-followers, which is exactly what you need at zero audience. Some posts that die on Bluesky will find an audience on Threads, and vice versa. No way to predict which — just cast wide.

Threads supports hashtags and they help. Add 2-3: #sustainability #business #operations

### Mastodon — "Same voice, different room"

Cross-post everything from Bluesky. Mastodon's culture is slightly more earnest and EU-heavy, so the contrarian stuff may land differently — some posts will perform better here, some worse. Don't optimize for it. Just be present.

One adjustment: Mastodon supports hashtags and they actually help discoverability there. Add 2-3 relevant hashtags to cross-posted content: #sustainability #SME #operations #circulareconomy

### Medium — "SEO shadow"

Copy-paste every Substack article to Medium 2-3 days after it publishes on Substack (so Substack gets the canonical URL and Medium doesn't cannibalize). Add relevant Medium tags. This is purely for search discoverability — people googling sustainability topics and finding your writing.

### Reddit — "The helpful stranger"

Not scheduled. Not batched. Just bookmark these subreddits and check in when you have 15 minutes:
- r/smallbusiness — SME owners asking real operational questions
- r/sustainability — sustainability discussions (quality varies)
- r/supplychain — supply chain professionals
- r/entrepreneur — business builders

When you see a question you can genuinely answer from experience, answer it. Be helpful first. If the book or framework is directly relevant, mention it naturally: "I wrote about this in more detail here: [link]." If it's not directly relevant, don't force it. Reddit rewards generosity and punishes self-promotion.

## The Term: Modern Sustainability

This is yours. You coined it. It doesn't exist in the market — there's "new sustainability" but not "modern sustainability." Own it.

**What it means:**
- Old sustainability = reports, certifications, virtue signaling, cost centers, compliance, moral obligation
- Modern sustainability = operational, profitable, sequential, built into how the business runs, capability-framed

**The hierarchy:**
- "Modern sustainability" = the category you're creating. The name for the shift.
- The 5 Stacks = the framework that implements modern sustainability.
- "Sustainability is the output, not the objective" = the thesis that explains why modern sustainability works.

**How to use it:**
- Drop it naturally. Don't define it every time — let context do the work.
- "This is what modern sustainability looks like." (under a good example)
- "Old sustainability gave you a report. Modern sustainability gives you a margin improvement."
- "Modern sustainability starts with operations, not ambition."
- Use it as a frame when commenting on current events: "This is old sustainability failing. Modern sustainability would have..."
- The book is literally called The Modern Sustainability Playbook. Every time you reference the book, the term reinforces itself.

**Goal:** Within 6 months, when someone googles "modern sustainability," you own the results.

## Content Pillars

Everything you post comes from one of six pillars. This keeps the content focused and makes batching easier.

### Pillar 0: MODERN SUSTAINABILITY
The term itself. The category you're creating. Old vs. modern. Every other pillar connects back to this.
- Substack: "What is modern sustainability?" as a foundational piece (consider making this Article 1 instead)
- LinkedIn: frame shifts — "old sustainability did X, modern sustainability does Y"
- Bluesky: the one-liner contrast. "Old sustainability: a report. Modern sustainability: a margin improvement."

### Pillar 1: THE REFRAME
Sustainability is the output, not the objective. Sustainability is just another way to say good business. The core positioning.
- Substack: deep exploration of the reframe and its implications
- LinkedIn: "you" version — speaking to someone who's been burned
- Bluesky: one-liner versions, quotable takes

### Pillar 2: THE STACKS
The framework itself. Each stack explained, the sequence, the compounding, the money framing.
- Substack: one stack per deep-dive article
- LinkedIn: "what if you could see where your money goes?" style posts, one stack at a time
- Bluesky: the money framing table, individual stack one-liners

### Pillar 3: THE BROKEN
What's wrong with current sustainability. Failed models, wasted money, empty reports, greenwashing, certification theater.
- Substack: teardown essays (category-level now, brand-specific later)
- LinkedIn: "you hired the consultant, nothing changed" pain validation
- Bluesky: quotes, stats, news commentary, hot takes

### Pillar 4: THE EVIDENCE
Data points, competitive gaps, market failures. The receipts.
- Substack: "the entire SME sustainability market is stuck at measurement" type analysis
- LinkedIn: surprising stats that reframe expectations
- Bluesky: single data points that shock — "73% of banks offer green SME financing. 2.8% of SMEs have applied."

### Pillar 5: THE GAP
What the market is missing. The white space. Why no one has built this before.
- Substack: competitive landscape analysis, what every platform gets wrong
- LinkedIn: "every sustainability tool does X. none does Y."
- Bluesky: short observations about what's missing

## Reactive Content — Current Events

The scheduled content is the backbone. But the posts that build your reputation fastest are reactive — your take on something that just happened. These can't be batched. They're the reason to check in for 10 minutes each morning even during autopilot weeks.

### What to react to:

- **Greenwashing exposures** — brand caught making false claims, ECGT enforcement actions, lawsuits. Your take: "This is what happens when sustainability is marketing, not operations."
- **CSRD / regulation news** — rollbacks, expansions, new requirements. Your take: "This is coming whether you're ready or not. Here's what it actually means for a 50-person company."
- **Corporate sustainability failures** — company cuts sustainability department, abandons net-zero pledge, publishes empty report. Your take: "The pattern again. Ambition without infrastructure."
- **Surprising data** — new studies, surveys, market reports on SME sustainability, green financing, circular economy. Your take: pull the one stat that shocks and frame it.
- **Pop culture / mainstream crossover** — sustainability shows up in unexpected places (TV, politics, viral moments). Your take: connect it back to the operational reality.
- **Good examples** — rare but important. When a company actually does it right, call that out too. Builds credibility that you're not just negative. Frame through the 5 Stacks: "This is what Stack 3 looks like in practice."

### Where reactive content goes:

| Type | Bluesky/Threads/Mastodon | LinkedIn | Substack |
|------|--------------------------|----------|----------|
| Quick take (1-2 lines) | Yes — this is the primary home | No | No |
| Hot take with context (a paragraph) | Yes | Yes, if it speaks to your audience's pain | No |
| Deep analysis | Share the headline + your angle | Standalone post if big enough | Bonus mid-week piece if it's genuinely significant |

### How it works during autopilot:

Your scheduled content posts automatically. When something happens in the news:
1. Bluesky/Threads/Mastodon: post a quick 1-2 line take immediately (2 minutes)
2. If it's big enough for LinkedIn: write a short "you" framed post (10 minutes)
3. If it's really significant: consider a bonus Substack piece (but only if it's worth 1,000+ words of original thinking)

Most weeks during autopilot: 0-2 reactive posts. Some weeks: none. That's fine. The scheduled content carries you. Reactive is bonus visibility when the moment is right.

### Sources to monitor:

Set up alerts or check these weekly:
- Google Alerts: "CSRD," "greenwashing," "sustainability SME," "circular economy EU"
- Bluesky feeds: follow sustainability journalists, EU policy accounts
- LinkedIn: your feed will surface relevant news once you start engaging
- catyeldi.com/essays — your own existing writing is a source when current events validate your points
