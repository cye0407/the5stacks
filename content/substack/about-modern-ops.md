**Modern Ops**
*The economics of good operations. For optimizers, not optimists.*

When costs rise, the easy move is to cut everything a little. Every department takes the same hit, whether it makes money or loses it.

A [Harvard study of 4,700 companies](https://hbsp.harvard.edu/product/R1003C-PDF-ENG) across three recessions found that only 9% came out clearly ahead of their competitors. What set them apart wasn't cutting harder. They cut selectively and kept investing in what worked. That takes knowing which product line, process and customer actually earns its keep.

That's what Modern Ops is about. Each essay takes one situation owners and operations leads actually face:

- Energy went up and you don't know which product is paying for it
- You have to cut 10% and don't know where
- One customer is 40% of your revenue
- You fixed a leak and it crept back

Optimists hope costs come back down. Optimizers find out what each part of the business actually costs and earns, then act on it. Each essay covers one problem, what it's likely costing you, and where to start. Sources are listed at the end.

Yes, well-run operations also waste less energy and material. Consider that a side effect, not the pitch.

**Who writes this**

I'm Cat. I've spent 10 years in product operations. Right now I'm bringing back to life a product its own company had stopped seeing the value of, by fixing how it runs rather than adding to it. Modern Ops is where I write down what I've learned about running lean without running blind.
