### SLIDE 7 — Worked Example: The Bakery
**Berlin Bakery — 14 employees**

Monthly output: ~18,000 loaves
Revenue: ~€54,000/month

*Let's trace where the money goes.*

---

### SLIDE 8 — Bakery: The Bill View
**What the owner sees every month:**

| Bill | Amount |
|---|---|
| Flour & ingredients | €11,200 |
| Electricity | €3,400 |
| Gas (ovens) | €2,100 |
| Rent | €4,800 |
| Staff | €19,600 |
| Packaging | €1,400 |
| Delivery/logistics | €2,800 |
| **Total** | **€45,300** |

Margin: ~€8,700 → **16.1%**

*Looks fine. But where's the risk?*

---

### SLIDE 9 — Bakery: The Split View
**Same data, reorganized by process:**

| Process | Monthly cost | Cost per loaf |
|---|---|---|
| Ingredients (flour, yeast, salt, butter) | €11,200 | €0.62 |
| Baking (energy: electricity + gas) | €5,500 | €0.31 |
| Labor (production staff only) | €14,200 | €0.79 |
| Packaging | €1,400 | €0.08 |
| Delivery | €2,800 | €0.16 |
| Overhead (rent, admin staff, misc) | €10,200 | €0.57 |
| **Total cost per loaf** | | **€2.53** |

Average selling price: €3.00 → **margin: €0.47/loaf**

---

### SLIDE 10 — Bakery: What the Split Reveals
**Three things the owner couldn't see from the bills:**

1. **Energy is 12% of cost** — and it's risen 35% in 18 months. That's €1,400/month more than two years ago. Invisible from the total bill.

2. **Delivery costs €0.16/loaf** — but only for wholesale. Direct-to-store loaves cost €0.04. The wholesale channel is 4x more expensive to serve.

3. **Oven scheduling** — two shifts overlap, running ovens at 40% capacity for 3 hours/day. That's ~€600/month in wasted gas.

*None of this shows up in a P&L. All of it shows up in your margin.*

### SLIDE 11 — The Cost Map Template
**Your turn. Here's the structure:**

```
INPUTS              PROCESSES           OUTPUTS
─────────           ──────────          ─────────
Energy              Process A           Product/Service X
Materials           Process B           Product/Service Y
Labor               Process C
Logistics
Facilities
```

For each input: What does it cost? Which process uses it? What output does it serve?

---

### SLIDE 12 — Cost-Per-Unit Formula
**Keep it simple:**

```
Total cost of inputs for Process A
÷ Number of units Process A produces
= Cost per unit
```

You don't need perfect data. A rough number is infinitely better than no number.

*If you know your monthly electricity bill and your monthly output, you already have enough.*

## Facilitator Notes

**Tone:** Practical, peer-level. Not a lecture. Ask questions, call out chat responses, make it feel like a conversation. If someone shares a number, work it with them live.

**Pacing:**
- Block 1 (Problem): Move fast. This is context, not content. They already feel the pain.
- Block 2 (Framework): This is the meat. Spend time on the bakery example — walk through it slowly. Let people ask questions.
- Block 3 (Exercise): Resist the urge to rush. 15 minutes feels long but people need it. Walk the room (virtually) — ask individuals how it's going.
- Block 4 (Share): This is where the magic happens. Peer insights are more powerful than anything you can say.
- Block 5 (Next Steps): Keep it tight. One action, resources, done.

**Tech:**
- Have slides in screen share, chat visible
- Mute participants during presentation blocks, unmute for exercise and share
- Drop links in chat at the end, not before (avoids distraction)
