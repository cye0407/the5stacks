# The 5 Stacks — Content Arc
## 26 Weeks. Three Phases. Five Platforms. One Sequence.

---

## The Inversion

Cat Yeldi builds TOWARD the book. Five Stacks builds FROM it.

The book is the entry point. The baseline tool is the first conversion. Workshops and courses are the revenue. Content exists to move readers along that path.

---

## The Audience Journey

The reader starts frustrated. They've been told sustainability matters — and they believe it — but every version of it they've been offered costs money, feels patronising, and changes nothing.

By Week 9, they should be thinking: *"I just built my own baseline from my own invoices. I can answer that questionnaire now. And there's financing I didn't know about."*

By Week 18, they should be thinking: *"The numbers are real. Companies in my sector are doing this. I want to try it."*

By Week 26, they should be: using the baseline tool, considering a workshop, and thinking of Five Stacks as their operating system.

---

## Platform Architecture

Each platform has one job:

| Platform | Job | Funnel |
|----------|-----|--------|
| **Medium** | Discovery — strangers find you through the algorithm | CTA at bottom → Substack |
| **LinkedIn** | Credibility + visual engagement — native content, business voice | Soft mention → Substack |
| **Bluesky** | Consumable bites — persona building, shareable takes | Bio → Substack |
| **Substack** | Email capture — the owned audience, the only list you control | IS the destination |
| **Website** | Book + tools — not a blog, link out to Substack/Medium/LinkedIn | Substack signup |

Everything funnels to Substack. But not by sharing Substack links everywhere. Each platform does its own job natively.

## Phase I: The Reframe (Weeks 1-9)

The reader starts frustrated with sustainability as it's been sold to them. They end with a practical sequence they've started to follow — and results they can already see.

### Movement 1: "Here's what actually happened" (Weeks 1-3)

Tone: Honest. Matter-of-fact. Relatable. Not angry, not cynical — just clear about what's true.

```
Week 1:   "Is sustainability as we know it really sustainable?"
           The system was built without you in the room.
           Academic → corporate → government → you're holding the bill.
           The reader recognises the history of what happened to them.

Week 2:   "Big business pushed it downhill."
           Supply chain questionnaires. Cost-shifting.
           You're doing their reporting. Figure it out or we won't choose you.
           The reader sees the structural squeeze.

Week 3:   "This is not an argument against the environment."
           Environmental health matters. The delivery system doesn't work.
           Environmental outcomes don't come from buying compliance.
           They come from running a better business.
           The reader gets the distinction: pro-environment, anti-broken-system.
```

By Week 3, the reader has language for what they've been feeling. They can explain it to someone else. They trust this voice.

### Movement 2: "There IS a better way" (Weeks 4-9)

Tone: Practical. Respectful. Each week builds on the last. No jargon. No stack numbers. Just "do this, then this, then this."

```
Week 4:   "Modern sustainability — what it actually looks like."
           Sustainability is the output, not the objective.
           A sequence where each step pays for the next.
           The reader sees the full picture for the first time.

Week 5:   "See what you actually have." (Stack 1 — Visibility)
           Your invoices already contain 80% of your baseline.
           The other 20% takes an afternoon.
           The reader starts tracking.

Week 6:   "Stop leaking money." (Stack 2 — Efficiency)
           You've been auto-paying bills you never examined.
           The average SME loses 5-15% to invisible inefficiencies.
           The reader finds money they were losing.

Week 7:   "That waste isn't waste." (Stack 3 — Circularity)
           What you pay to throw away, someone else would buy.
           NISP: 15,000 companies, £1B saved, 47M tonnes diverted.
           The reader sees waste as a revenue line.

Week 8:   "Know what breaks you." (Stack 4 — Resilience)
           Map your dependencies. Find your single points of failure.
           An afternoon with a whiteboard.
           The reader maps their risks for the first time.

Week 9:   "Now prove it." (Stack 5 — Regeneration)
           €789B in green financing. 2.8% of SMEs apply.
           You don't need to be the greenest. You need to be the most provable.
           The reader can now answer the questionnaire from Week 2.
```

**The Reader at Week 9:**

They've been through 9 weeks. They've seen:

- An honest account of why sustainability failed them (Weeks 1-3)
- A different approach that starts with what they already have (Week 4)
- A practical sequence they've started following (Weeks 5-9)
- A callback to the questionnaire that felt impossible — now answerable (Week 9 → Week 2)

They can't argue this is theory. They've started doing it.

**They're ready for the proof.**

Phase II begins: "You've seen the sequence. Now here's the evidence — from businesses like yours, in euros."

## Phase II: The Proof (Weeks 10-18)

The reader has the framework. Now they need evidence it works — in their sector, in their currency, for businesses their size.

### Movement 3: "The numbers are real" (Weeks 10-13)

Every piece has a number in it. Shift from concepts to evidence.

```
Week 10:  "The €47K nobody noticed leaving."
           An energy efficiency case study with real numbers.

Week 11:  "What NISP proved about waste at scale."
           £1B saved across 15,000 companies. Not theory. Not a pilot.

Week 12:  "The SME that answered a 47-page questionnaire in one afternoon."
           Callback to Week 2. Visibility → provability in practice.

Week 13:  "Why your carbon number is lying to you."
           Metrics vs. operations. KPI theater. What to measure instead.
```

### Movement 4: "This works in your sector" (Weeks 14-17)

Go vertical. The reader sees their own industry.

```
Week 14:  "Five Stacks for manufacturing — where the money actually is."

Week 15:  "Five Stacks for food & beverage — from waste cost to revenue."

Week 16:  "Five Stacks for professional services — the stack everyone skips."

Week 17:  "Five Stacks vs. B Corp / ISO / EOS — what they assume you have."
```

### Movement 5: "See it in action" (Weeks 18)

Bridge to implementation.

```
Week 18:  "From baseline to action plan — the first 90 days."
           Implementation roadmap. Workshop tease. Clear next step.
```

**The Reader at Week 18:**

"Five Stacks isn't theory. Companies in my sector are saving real money. I want to try it."

## Production Notes

- Each essay: 1500-2500 words. One theme. Practical where possible. No jargon.
- Register: Honest, direct, relatable. Write for Jim who owns 3 tire shops and Svenja who manages a sewing company. They've been talked down to. Don't do that.
- Framework language: The word "stack" never appears in reader-facing content during Phase I. The framework is present. The labels are absent. Let readers discover the system by doing it.
- Visual assets: Every piece needs platform-appropriate visuals. Carousels for LinkedIn. Quote cards for Bluesky. Headers for Medium/Substack. Budget one design session per batch of content.
- The environment matters: Every piece that critiques the current model must make clear — environmental health is important. This is an argument about approach, not about whether the planet matters.
