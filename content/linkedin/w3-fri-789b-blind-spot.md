The sustainability industry has a $789 billion blind spot.

73% of financial institutions now offer green financing for SMEs. Sustainability-linked loans. Green credit lines. Preferential rates for businesses that can demonstrate environmental improvement.

2.8% of SMEs have applied.

Read that again. Nearly three-quarters of banks are offering cheaper money to businesses that can prove their sustainability credentials. Fewer than 3 in 100 SMEs have even tried to access it.

That's a $789 billion green growth opportunity sitting on the table. ICC and Sage research. Not a projection — an identified gap.

Why?

Because no tool connects the dots.

You have carbon calculators that give you a number. You have reporting platforms that produce a document. You have certification bodies that give you a badge.

But nobody builds the bridge between "here's what you improved" and "here's the financing you now qualify for." Nobody takes your documented efficiency gains — the 15% energy reduction, the waste stream you diverted, the baseline you built — and turns them into a financing application.

The data exists. The financing exists. The connection doesn't.

Think about what this means for your business. You've already reduced energy costs. You've already documented your baseline. You've already improved your waste management. Those improvements aren't just operational wins. They're evidence. Evidence that a growing number of lenders want to see — and will reward with better terms.

But nobody told you that. Because the sustainability industry is built to measure and report, not to connect your operational proof to the capital that's looking for it.

The blind spot isn't the money. The money is there. The blind spot is that nobody shows SMEs the path from operational improvement to financial advantage.
