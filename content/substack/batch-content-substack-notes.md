## Week 1 — "Sustainability Has a Reputation Problem"

### Article Promo (publish day)

The sustainability model most companies follow was designed backwards. Start with the destination, and everything becomes a cost. Start with operations, and everything becomes a margin improvement. I wrote about why the ambition was never the problem — the infrastructure was.

### General Topic Note 1

A fifty-thousand-euro sustainability report that nobody reads is not a sustainability strategy. It's a receipt.

### General Topic Note 2

The pattern is so consistent it should be a template: announce the commitment, hire the consultant, produce the PDF, quietly shelve it at the next budget review. The problem was never motivation. It was sequencing. Modern sustainability starts with operations, not ambition.

### General Topic Note 3

Sustainability is just another way to say good business. The moment you accept that, the economics flip. It stops being something you fund and becomes something that funds itself.

## Week 2 — "You Can't Fix Leaks You Can't See"

### Article Promo (publish day)

It's Thursday afternoon. A sustainability questionnaire lands in your inbox. Scope 1, Scope 2, Scope 3, energy by source, waste by type. It's due Friday. You don't have the numbers. Neither does your competitor — but the one who does gets to keep the contract. New this week: why the baseline is the most underrated capability in business.

### General Topic Note 1

Two suppliers. Similar price, similar quality. One sends a link to verified baseline data within the hour. The other needs two weeks to pull numbers together. That difference is already showing up in procurement decisions.

### General Topic Note 2

Most SMEs have 15-30% waste hidden in their operations that nobody is tracking. Not because anyone is careless — because nobody had the data to see it. Compressed air leaking into ceilings. Lighting running in empty rooms. Slow drains that compound into serious money over a year.

### General Topic Note 3

Here's the part that changes everything: the data you need for CSRD compliance is the exact same data that shows you where you're bleeding money. Build the baseline for one and you get both. Compliance and operational intelligence are the same data set, viewed through different lenses.

## Week 3 — "Fix Leaks Before Adding Initiatives"

### Article Promo (publish day)

Every carbon accounting tool on the market will give you a number. "Your emissions are 142 tCO2e." Great. Now what? The entire SME sustainability software market is built around measurement. Nobody builds what comes after. This week: why fixing leaks beats adding initiatives, every time.

### General Topic Note 1

The five stacks in money terms: See your money. Save money. Keep more money. Protect your money. Find more money. Notice what's missing from that list? The word "sustainability." It doesn't need to be there. It's the output, not the objective.

### General Topic Note 2

The right model for efficiency-as-carbon-reduction already exists. Carbmee built it — MACC curves that rank actions from cheapest to most expensive per tonne reduced. Brilliant. Enterprise-only. Starting above 50,000 euros a year. The 99% of European businesses that actually need it get nothing.

### General Topic Note 3

Efficiency isn't a sustainability initiative. It's good business. When energy prices spike, the company using 25% more energy per unit than it needs feels the hit 25% harder. Modern sustainability doesn't start with saving the planet. It starts with stopping the leaks.

## Week 4 — "What You Discard Is Lost Margin"

### Article Promo (publish day)

Between 2005 and 2016, a UK programme called NISP generated one billion pounds in new sales and 1.1 billion in cost reductions — just by asking companies: "Does anyone need what you're throwing away?" Then the government defunded it. Nobody built the digital replacement. This week: why what you discard is lost margin.

### General Topic Note 1

Every waste stream in your operation has two costs. The obvious one: disposal. The hidden one: the value inside it. Material that could be someone else's input. Heat that could warm another process. Byproducts you're literally paying to throw into a landfill. Flip that frame, and the numbers look very different.

### General Topic Note 2

70% of manufacturers expect circular solutions to increase revenue by 2027. 45% haven't scaled a single circular initiative. The gap isn't motivation — it's the inability to see the financial case before committing. That's the piece nobody has built for SMEs yet.

### General Topic Note 3

Stacks 1 and 2 are internal — your own data, your own leaks, your own operations. Stack 3 is where the walls come down. One company's waste becomes another company's input. That's where modern sustainability stops being a philosophy and starts being visible in real transactions.
