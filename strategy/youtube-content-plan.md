# YouTube Content Plan — The 5 Stacks

*March 2026*

---

## Position

Pro-environment. Anti-broken-industry. Pro-small-business.

"The environment matters. The industry built to 'fix' it is a wealth transfer from small to large. Real sustainability is good operations."

This is not theory. This is people's lives.

---

## Format

- Screen + voiceover (no face on camera — slides, diagrams, screen recordings)
- Honest, direct, relatable. Written for Jim (3 tire shops) and Svenja (sewing company).
- Every claim sourced. Not ranting — revealing.
- No jargon. No academic tone. Well-educated but not ivory tower.

---

## Content Phases

### Phase 1: "The Foundation"

**7 standalone videos, 5-7 min each. Screen + voiceover. No products. No selling.**

Goal: Establish credibility and trust. Pure education. No products. No opinion. No selling.
Tone: Teacher. Calm, clear, respectful.
Viewer leaves thinking: "This person knows what they're talking about."

**Video 1 — The Physics**
- Not preachy. Cause and effect.
- Systems thinking: feedback loops, thresholds, tipping points.
- Stock vs flow. Not "the planet is dying" — "here's how the system works, physically."

**Video 2 — The History + The Lenses**
- How did "pollution is bad" become a $30B compliance industry?
- Pre-American history: indigenous practices, Edo Japan, medieval commons, Carlowitz 1713.
- The arc: environmental science > policy > corporate adoption > frameworks > industry.
- The different lenses: academia, the haters, corporate, alphabet soup.
- Scopes decoded in plain language.

**Video 3 — THE TRAP: Why Nobody Moves First**
- Game theory through everyday examples. You want to do the right thing — but if you do and nobody else does, you just made your life harder.
- Svenja's sewing company: invest in better operations, get undercut by a competitor who didn't bother.
- The system's response: questionnaires. But forms don't fix the math.
- Anchor: "When the first mover loses, nobody moves."

**Video 4 — THE SETUP: Who Profits From the Paperwork**
- An entire industry built on sustainability that doesn't make anything sustainable.
- Green labels, rating agencies, questionnaires pushed downhill. Who pays, who profits.
- A company with a polished report scores higher than one with great operations and no report.
- Anchor: "The system rewards good reports. Not good operations."

**Video 5 — THE LONG GAME: The Critics Are Right. For About 18 Months.**
- Short-term vs long-term thinking. The dentist analogy. The critics are right — for a quarter.
- Svenja found €400/month leaking from equipment running when nobody was there.
- The companies that know their numbers aren't doing sustainability. They're doing business.
- Anchor: "The sustainability is just what you call it afterward."

**Video 6 — Modern Sustainability**
- The reframe: sustainability is the output of running a business well.
- The sequence: see what you have → stop leaking money → that waste isn't waste → know what breaks you → prove it.
- Each step pays for the next.

**Video 7 — What This Means for You**
- What you do on Monday morning.
- Don't think about compliance. Think about operations.
- The questionnaire is a byproduct of knowing your business.

CTA: "Subscribe if this made sense to you." Nothing else.

### Phase 2: "The Machine"

**Individual videos, each with a story + on-screen sources**

Goal: Ask the uncomfortable questions. Follow the money. Who owns what. Who profits. Who pays.
Tone: Investigative journalist. Measured but pointed.
Earned the right to go here because Phase 1 proved competence.

After the first 1-2 videos in this phase, soft product mentions become natural.

Video ideas:
- "The private equity firms that own your ESG rating" (EcoVadis / CVC / BlackRock)
- "CDP claims to be a non-profit. Here's who's behind the $100 trillion curtain."
- "Your customer pays EcoVadis to rate you. You pay EcoVadis to see your own score. Who wins?"
- "The same investors funding sustainability platforms own stakes in the companies causing the damage."
- "Big companies get ESG awards. You get the questionnaire."
- "CSRD: how the EU made big business's problem your problem."
- "You've never produced a microplastic. Why are you filling out this form?"

---

### Phase 3: "The People"

**Use-case videos. Defined personas. Problem > solution.**

Goal: The viewer sees themselves. Each video starts with a real scenario, walks through the stakes, arrives at a resolution.
Tone: Guide. "Here's what I'd do if I were you."
Products are part of the story — not bolted on.

Video ideas:
- Jim got a 47-page questionnaire from his biggest customer. What does he do?
- Svenja's sewing company needs to report energy consumption. Where does she start?
- A 40-person manufacturer is told they need an EcoVadis score. What does that actually cost them?
- A food & bev supplier gets asked about Scope 3 for the first time.
- A professional services firm gets a sustainability clause in a new contract.

Each video: real scenario, real stakes, problem to resolution.

---

### Phase 4: "The Deep Dives"

**In-depth breakdowns of specific frameworks, regulations, industries**

Goal: Authoritative reference content. The stuff people search for and come back to.
Tone: Expert. "Let me show you everything."
By now the audience trusts you enough to sit through 20-30 minutes of detail.

Video ideas:
- Full EcoVadis breakdown: what it measures, what it misses, what it costs, who it serves
- CSRD: what it actually requires and what it doesn't
- Scope 3: why it's the thing everyone gets wrong
- CDP questionnaire: a complete walkthrough
- Sector-specific: manufacturing, food & bev, professional services
- ISO 14001 vs. actually reducing your environmental impact
- The real cost of B Corp certification
