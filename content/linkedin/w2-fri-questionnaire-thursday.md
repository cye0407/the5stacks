The questionnaire lands in your inbox on a Thursday afternoon. It's due Friday.

You recognise this moment. Your biggest customer — the one that accounts for a third of your revenue — needs sustainability data for their CSRD reporting. They need your Scope 1 and 2 emissions. Your energy sources. Your waste destinations. Your material sourcing. A methodology note explaining how you calculated everything.

By tomorrow.

You don't have any of this ready. Not because you don't care. Because it was never set up.

So you start pulling invoices. You're digging through emails to the waste hauler. You're guessing at energy splits between gas and electric. You're estimating waste volumes from skip dimensions. You're putting numbers in a spreadsheet with the quiet understanding that if anyone scrutinised them, they wouldn't hold up.

Your operations lead is annoyed because this is the third time this year. Your finance person is confused because the numbers don't match what they have. Your MD wants to know why this takes two days every time it happens.

And the worst part? The company down the supply chain — the one your customer will switch to if you can't keep up — they sent their data back in an hour. Because they built a baseline once and update it monthly.

Same question. Same deadline. Completely different experience.

This is what it looks like when sustainability is treated as a fire drill instead of infrastructure. Every questionnaire is a panic. Every response is approximate. Every scramble costs more than the system that would have prevented it.

Two hours a month. That's what a baseline takes to maintain. The questionnaire that used to ruin your Friday becomes a 20-minute task.

You already have the data. It's in your invoices, your utility bills, your purchase orders. It just isn't organised.
