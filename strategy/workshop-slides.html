The Framework · 0:26

What the split reveals

Finding 1

Energy is 12% of cost — risen 35% in 18 months. That’s €1,400/month more than two years ago. Invisible from the total bill.

Finding 2

Delivery costs €0.16/loaf — but only for wholesale. Direct-to-store costs €0.04. The wholesale channel is 4x more expensive to serve.

Finding 3

Oven scheduling — two shifts overlap, running ovens at 40% capacity for 3 hours/day. ~€600/month in wasted gas.

None of this shows up in a P&L. All of it shows up in your margin.

The Framework · 0:28

The Cost Map

INPUTS PROCESSES OUTPUTS ————— ————— ————— Energy → Process A → Product / Service X Materials → Process B → Product / Service Y Labor → Process C Logistics Facilities

For each input: What does it cost? Which process uses it? What output does it serve?

The Framework · 0:29

Cost-Per-Unit

Total cost of inputs for Process A ÷ Units Process A produces —————————— = Cost per unit

You don’t need perfect data.
A rough number is infinitely better than no number.

If you know your monthly electricity bill and your monthly output, you already have enough.

Your Turn · 0:30

15 minutes. Here’s what to do.

  1. List your top 5 cost categories — energy, materials, labor, logistics, rent
  2. Pick one core process — the thing your business does most often
  3. Estimate the cost-per-unit — What goes in? What does it cost? How many units come out?
  4. Flag the blind spots — which costs do you NOT know the breakdown of?

Use paper, a spreadsheet, whatever works. I’ll be here if you get stuck.

Your Turn · On Screen During Exercise

If you’re stuck, start here

  • Has it changed in the last 12 months? By how much?
  • Do you know what it costs per unit of output?
  • Which bill do you dread opening — and why?
  • Is there a cost you pay every month that you’ve never broken down?
