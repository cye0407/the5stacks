# The 5 Stacks — Content Plan
## Platform Strategy, Scheduling, Production & Tracking

---

## Platform Architecture

Each platform has one job. None of them are interchangeable.

### Medium — Discovery Engine

**Job:** Strangers find you here. Medium's algorithm surfaces content to people who don't know you. Google indexes Medium well. This is your top-of-funnel.

**What you publish:** The full anchor essay, every week. This is where the best version lives.

**What you don't do:** Build community here. Medium followers are borrowed — you never see their email. Medium owns that relationship.

**Funnel:** Every article ends with a CTA to Substack. Your Medium bio links to Substack. You're renting this audience to grow the one you own.

**Voice:** Same as Substack. Honest, direct, practical. Medium title may differ from Substack title for discoverability (SEO/algorithmic optimisation).

**Cadence:** 1x/week, published Tuesday.

**Tags:** Use Medium's 5-tag system. Always include: sustainability, small-business, operations. Rotate the other 2 based on topic.

---

### LinkedIn — Visual Credibility

**Job:** Professional audience engagement. Native visual content in a business voice. This is where credibility compounds — reposts from your target audience, comments that lead to relationships, carousels that get saved.

**What you publish:** Standalone visual content — carousels, infographics, data graphics, quote cards. Each post delivers value on its own. It does NOT say "go read my article."

**What you don't do:** Share links. LinkedIn's algorithm penalises external links — drops reach 50-80%. If you mention Substack, do it as a soft line at the end or in the comments.

**Funnel:** Soft. "I write about this weekly — link in comments" or "More at [substack] every Tuesday." Never the main event.

**Voice:** Business voice. Start with "you" not "I." Validate their experience, then reframe it. Never "I'm excited to announce." Never lecture.

**Cadence:** 3x/week. Monday / Wednesday / Friday.

**Format priority:**
1. Carousels (PDF uploads) — highest engagement
2. Image posts with text — strong engagement
3. Text-only — decent with a strong hook
4. Link posts — worst reach, avoid

---

### Bluesky — Personality Channel

**Job:** Build persona. Shareable bites. The place where people follow you because you're interesting, not because you're selling something.

**What you publish:** Short takes (<300 characters ideal). Mix of:
- Honest observations about sustainability's failures
- Data points that surprise ("€789B available, 2.8% apply")
- Pop culture or everyday analogies
- Occasional links to Medium pieces

**What you don't do:** Sell. Hard-link to products. Post threads that are just a chopped-up article.

**Funnel:** Bio links to Substack (or the5stacks.com which has Substack link). The posts themselves are self-contained.

**Voice:** Sharp, short, quotable. Honest, not angry. Pop culture meets business when it fits. The person at the dinner party who says the thing everyone was thinking.

**Cadence:** 1-2x/day from batched content. Reactive when news breaks.

**No hashtags.** Bluesky culture doesn't use them.

---

### Substack — The Destination

**Job:** Email capture. The owned audience. The only list you control. When Medium shuts down or LinkedIn changes its algorithm, Substack subscribers are still yours.

**What you publish:** The same anchor essay as Medium + a Note promoting it. The essay lives here as the permanent home. Over time, Substack becomes where the relationship deepens — personal notes, reader Q&A, community posts.

**Funnel:** This IS the destination. Everything else points here.

**Voice:** "The one who did the work." Teacher who respects your intelligence. No jargon unless immediately explained. Write like a smart friend who runs a business.

**Cadence:** 1x/week, published Wednesday (one day after Medium — gives Medium the first-mover algorithmic advantage). Substack Notes: 1-2x/week promoting the essay + topic-relevant observations.

---

### Website (the5stacks.com) — Book + Tools Hub

**Job:** The static home. Book download. Baseline tool link. Framework overview. Not a blog. Not a content destination.

**What lives here:**
- Landing page with book download CTA (LemonSqueezy)
- Link to baseline tool (baseline.the5stacks.com)
- Framework overview page (the sequence, in plain language)
- "Read more" section with 3 links: Substack, Medium, LinkedIn
- Eventually: workshop page, course page

**What doesn't live here:** Articles. Blog posts. A content feed. The website is a hub, not a publication.

**Updates:** Per-phase, not weekly. Phase I: ensure book + baseline are linked. Phase II: add framework page. Phase III: add workshop/course page.

## Content Types

### 1. Anchor Essays (Medium + Substack)

The primary content unit. One per week. 1500-2500 words. One theme. Defines the week's content across all platforms.

Production: batch-write 2-4 weeks ahead. Never publish from a blank week.

### 2. LinkedIn Posts (Native Visual)

3 per week. Each derived from the week's theme but stands alone — different angle, different format, different hook. A reader who never sees your essay should still get value from LinkedIn alone.

Formats:
- **Carousels:** 5-8 slides. Best for step-by-step explanations, comparisons, checklists. Budget 1 carousel per week.
- **Infographics/data graphics:** Single image with a stat or comparison. Budget 1-2 per week.
- **Text posts with image:** Shorter text (~150 words) with a supporting graphic or quote card.

Production: extract 3 angles from the anchor essay. Design visuals. Schedule Mon/Wed/Fri.

### 3. Bluesky Bites

10-15 per week. Mix of:
- **Theme-related:** 2-3 bites pulled from the week's anchor essay
- **Evergreen batches:** Pre-written posts from 6 categories:
  1. Modern vs. old sustainability
  2. The broken model
  3. Data bombs (surprising stats)
  4. Five Stacks framework (in plain language)
  5. Pop culture crossover
  6. Substack/book teasers
- **Reactive:** News-driven, same-day posts when something relevant happens

Production: batch 60+ posts. Schedule 1-2/day. Add reactive posts as needed.

### 4. Substack Notes

1-2 per week. Promote the essay + share observations. Short, conversational. Not cross-posted content — native to the Notes format.

### 5. Medium Cross-Post

1x/week. Same essay as Substack, published one day earlier. Medium title may differ for discoverability.

## Batching Strategy

### The Discipline

Never publish from a blank week. Always have 2-4 weeks of content banked.

### The Process

**Phase 1: Write**
Sprint-write 2-4 anchor essays in one sitting. Don't edit. Don't design. Just write.

**Phase 2: Edit**
Edit essays for voice consistency. Check: does this sound like it's written for Jim and Svenja? Remove any jargon that crept in. Remove any sentence that talks down.

**Phase 3: Extract**
From each essay, extract:
- 3 LinkedIn angles (different hooks, different formats)
- 2-3 Bluesky bites (the most quotable lines)
- 1 Substack Note (the "why I wrote this" or the most provocative question)

**Phase 4: Design**
Design visual assets for LinkedIn posts. Use templates. Budget one design session per batch (not per post).

**Phase 5: Schedule**
Schedule everything. Medium (Tue), Substack (Wed), LinkedIn (Mon/Wed/Fri), Bluesky (daily from queue).

**Phase 6: Engage**
Respond to comments. This is where relationships start. Not optional.

## What NOT to Publish

- The word "stack" in reader-facing content during Phase I. The framework is present. The labels are absent.
- Jargon without immediate explanation. If you say "circularity," follow it with "turning a cost line into a revenue line."
- Anything that talks down to the reader. Jim and Svenja have been condescended to by the sustainability industry. Don't continue that tradition.
- Hard sells. The CTA is always soft: "the book is free at the5stacks.com" or a Substack link. Never "BUY MY THING."
- Climate denial energy. Every piece that critiques the system must make clear: the environment matters. The delivery system doesn't work.

## Metrics That Matter

### Track
- **Substack subscribers** — your owned audience. The number that matters most.
- **Medium reads** — your discovery engine. Are strangers finding you?
- **LinkedIn engagement** — comments and reposts from target audience (not vanity likes).
- **Book downloads** — via LemonSqueezy dashboard.
- **Baseline tool signups** — organic traffic from content.
- **Inbound inquiries** — the ultimate signal. Someone reaching out because they read your stuff.

### Don't Track (or at least don't optimise for)
- Likes / claps — vanity
- Follower counts — vanity
- Post impressions — misleading
- Viral moments — unsustainable

### Review Cadence
- **Weekly:** Glance at subscriber count and Medium reads. Note what landed.
- **Quarter mark (Week 9):** Full review. Platform performance, audience quality, engagement patterns. Adjust Phase II themes if needed.
- **Halfway (Week 13):** Strategy review. Is the reader journey working? Adjust Phase III.
- **Phase end (Week 26):** Full retrospective. What worked, what didn't, what to change for the next 26 weeks.

Five Stacks content occasionally touches other CY brands. Rules:

- **Cat Yeldi essays** (catyeldi.com): Cross-reference when the operational competence thesis connects. Link naturally, don't force it.
- **Ecosystems United:** Don't mention until Phase III. Ecosystems United is the platform implementation — reveal it when the reader is ready for tools.
